A CVSS-only sort ranks three non-KEV findings among the top 12; the risk score ranks none. The sample has 12 KEV and 8 non-KEV findings, all synthetic.

Everything here runs on synthetic data. The sample uses real KEV CVE IDs with invented assets, criticality ratings, EPSS, CVSS and ages. It shows how the ordering behaves, not what any real environment looks like. No DHS or CBP data is involved.

CVSS says how bad a flaw could be, not whether anyone is using it. On this sample a CVSS-only sort puts 9 KEV findings in its top 12. The average CVSS of the two groups differs by 1.19, and the average EPSS by 0.906.

CVSS scores the severity of a flaw from 0 to 10. KEV is CISA's list of CVEs known to be exploited in the wild.

Run on 2026-11-10 from a fresh clone (commit 597c1f7): 9 tests pass. The risk score puts all 12 KEV findings in its top 12.

The script reads a CSV of findings, matches each CVE against a KEV file in the CISA feed format, and scores it with the same formula as kevgate: KEV 0.40, asset criticality 0.25, EPSS 0.20, CVSS 0.20, normalized to 0 to 100. Ties break on EPSS, then CVSS. Output is text, CSV or JSON, plus a KEV versus non-KEV comparison.

The EPSS and asset-criticality terms and all the weights are this project's own design. They are not inputs of CISA BOD 26-04, which ties urgency to KEV status, exposure, automatability and technical impact.

  • Synthetic data, so the results say nothing about real remediation.
  • It uses CVSS and EPSS values you supply; it does not query NVD or the EPSS API. Live mode fetches only the KEV catalog.
  • A weighted sum cannot express a hard rule such as "KEV and internet-exposed always first".
  • No per-finding due dates against BOD 26-04; age is reported as an average per group.
  • The weights are a starting point and have not been validated against outcomes.
